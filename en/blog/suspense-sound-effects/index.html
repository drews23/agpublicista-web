Use suspense effects in an edit

A sound can anticipate a reveal, accompany it, or give way to silence. Choose its role before adding the WAV:

  1. Test anticipation. Place strings or a crescendo before a shot change. Adjust the lead-in to the scene rather than giving every file the same offset.
  2. Start with one impact. Listen to El Boom over the music before adding another layer. If layering, balance levels and check definition and clipping.
  3. Leave room before the hit. Try two or three quiet seconds of strings or the dark swell before the Boom, creating contrast rather than constant loudness.
  4. Automate gain. Bring the tension layer up before the cut, then lower it as the impact arrives.
  5. Check on a phone speaker. If the impact only works on headphones, choose a clearer attack or lower the surrounding music. Pitching down an octave adds bass a small speaker may not reproduce; it is not a complete fix.

In Premiere Pro, keep effects on separate tracks and use volume keyframes. In CapCut, inspect the waveform peak to align the Boom with the cut. In DaVinci Resolve, use Fairlight to automate a crossfade between tension and impact. For a visual accent, compare the After Effects transitions and titles (Spanish guide); a flash combined with the Boom is one possible treatment.

Exercise: reveal an image without covering the voice

Try Suspenso Oscuro Oleaje.wav before a reveal and El Boom.wav on the reveal itself. This is a proposed combination: adjust after listening.

  1. Mark the reveal and the narrator's final word on separate tracks.
  2. Introduce the swell before the reveal and reduce it during speech.
  3. Leave space between the phrase and impact. Compare a Boom version with a silent version.
  4. Choose by intent: an impact emphasizes arrival; silence lets the image hold the tension.
  5. Check headphones and speakers so the tail does not cover the next line.
