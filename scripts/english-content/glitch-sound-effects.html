How to place a glitch in your edit

  1. Align the audible attack. Zoom into the waveform and locate the crackle or transient you want to synchronize. The file's starting edge and its audible impact may be different points. Test whether an early attack works as a warning or a later one changes the feel of the cut.
  2. Use glitches as punctuation. Try one at the opening, another at a meaningful section change, and perhaps one at the end. Repeating the same interruption at every cut makes it harder to tell which change matters.
  3. Trim without adding clicks. Keep the attack and shorten the tail only when it overlaps the next shot or a spoken word. Apply a short fade at the new endpoint and listen on headphones.

For a quick visual pairing, duplicate the video clip at the glitch frame, offset it by 2 or 3 pixels, and experiment with the blend mode. Compare the result with and without the sound.

Keep effects on a separate track from speech. Adjust the level while listening to both tracks together: a fixed gain reduction does not guarantee intelligible dialogue. Check the output meter for clipping. Markers and a zoomed-in timeline in Premiere Pro, CapCut, or DaVinci Resolve help you compare the attack with the chosen frame.

Exercise: an interruption between two sentences

Import (3) Bass Glitch.wav and Glitch de entrada.wav as two alternatives. Original filenames remain unchanged in the archive.

  1. Place a marker between the last word of one shot and the first word of the next.
  2. Align an audible attack with that marker, testing one file at a time. Trim the tail if it masks the next sentence.
  3. Compare the sequence with the effect enabled and disabled at the same playback volume. Keep the version that leaves both sentences clear.

For a calm tutorial, reserve the glitch for a specific error or state change. Pair it with the After Effects transition and title template when your edit needs a matching visual treatment.
