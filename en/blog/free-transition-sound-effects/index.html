How to synchronize a transition

  • Whooshes at cuts: locate the sound's strongest emphasis and align it with the shot change. The lead-in depends on the attack; moving every clip by the same number of frames does not produce the same synchronization.
  • Risers before a larger change: a rising sound can prepare the next section. The source guide suggests trying a buildup of 1 or 2 seconds with suitable magic or cybernetic sounds. Listen to the actual file and retain the preparation it needs.
  • Keep speech readable: listen to the transition and dialogue together. Shorten or lower a tail that masks a word, and avoid an abrupt level jump at the cut.

Listen to the full edit once on headphones and once on a phone speaker. Adjust effects that dominate or disappear on either device. Check the output meter before exporting.

Exercise: choose a sound for a section change

For a tutorial, compare one wind transition and the cybernetic transition over the same change from introduction to demonstration. Treat them as alternatives, not layers you must stack.

  1. Mark the visual cut and locate the audible emphasis in each WAV.
  2. Align each alternative with the cut, retaining part of its buildup if needed.
  3. Listen to the first instruction after the cut. Shorten or lower the tail if it makes that instruction harder to understand.
  4. Keep a marker at the chosen synchronization point so you can reuse the timing without confusing the file edge with its audible attack.
