Synchronize sound with the image

Keep effects on a separate track from voice and music. You can then adjust level, position, and tails independently. Zoom into the waveform and listen before aligning: the file's start does not always coincide with its strongest emphasis.

  1. Mark the visual event. It could be the completed logo or the frame where a title disappears.
  2. Locate the WAV's emphasis. Try a few frames before and after the marker. Choose the version that makes sound and image feel connected.
  3. Adjust the tail. Use a fade-out if you need to shorten it. Start short and extend it if a cut remains audible.
  4. Mix with dialogue present. Lower the effect until it supports the message without covering words. Check its sum with music: tracks that sound fine separately can clip together.

There is no single level for every video. Watch output meters and test on headphones and a phone speaker. If the effect only registers when excessively loud, try another file or position before increasing its level again.

Exercise: an entrance and exit in eight seconds

This is an editing exercise, not a visual template included in the RAR. Use a simple title or the After Effects text templates (Spanish guide).

  • 0–2 seconds: reveal “New tutorial.” Try Brillante or Ensueño, aligning its emphasis with the completed entrance.
  • 2–5 seconds: allow time to read. Keep the title stable; add no effect unless a new action occurs.
  • 5–8 seconds: close the scene with a short fade sound under the text's exit. Align its ending and adjust excess tail.

Export two versions: one aligned to the start of each animation and one aligned to its main visual event. Compare without looking at the timeline to distinguish perceived synchronization from matching clip starts.
